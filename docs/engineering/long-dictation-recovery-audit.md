# Long dictation recovery audit

Updated 2026-10-19. This document separates implemented repairs from their validation and remaining acceptance gates. The audit used source inspection and synthetic fixtures; it did not inspect user audio, preferences or credentials.

## Implemented repairs

- **Final ASR errors are explicit.** Parakeet batch/stream finalization no longer presents a failed decode's preview as successful final text. `lastTranscriptionError` survives Stop so AppState takes its recovery path; the available preview remains incomplete recovery material. A generation-latched failure also catches earlier live ingestion/processing errors, even when a later final call nominally succeeds. Normal stop cancellation is distinguished from unexpected decoder cancellation.
- **Pending-queue overflow cannot silently succeed.** The 120-second pending-ASR cap remains, but any dropped samples cause explicit incomplete-recognition failure at finalization. Both that counter and the processing-failure latch reset for a new capture generation.
- **Late completed recognition remains a cancellation checkpoint.** A noncooperative decoder returning a valid final result after cancellation can supply the optional recovery checkpoint. The revoked AppState session token still prevents history/output effects. A thrown cancellation/error or incomplete input does not become a completed checkpoint.
- **History-enabled sessions require a safety spool.** Local recording now starts recovery audio when History is enabled, even if the user has disabled retention of deliberately cancelled sessions. Failure to create that spool blocks recording instead of promising unavailable recovery. History-off continues to prohibit recovery audio. An intentional cancellation may discard its temporary spool according to the captured preservation setting; unexpected ASR/storage failures preserve it.
- **Writer admission is bounded and ordered.** Recovery audio permits at most 30 seconds of queued mono 16 kHz samples. Admission and enqueue share a lock with `finish`, so closing cannot overtake an accepted chunk. Writes remain on the serial writer queue. Queue overflow or a disk-write error stops further admission and exposes a static partial-recovery error. The recording poller observes that error and requests Stop on a fresh task.
- **Stop checks the final writer result.** After final recognition, AppState explicitly awaits `capture.finish()` and checks its error before classifying the session as successful or deleting audio. This covers a last-buffer failure occurring after the polling task stopped. Successfully completed recognition can be retained alongside a partial audio spool.
- **Metadata failure keeps existing recovery material.** Preservation closes/drains the CAF before publishing metadata. A metadata-write failure retains the CAF and an in-process volatile entry containing known preview/completed text; it does not call destructive discard. Reload retains those volatile entries in the current process.
- **Orphan audio is discoverable.** Startup/reload validates regular CAF files without metadata, checks their 16 kHz mono format and positive frame count, and offers an interrupted recovery entry with an explicit completeness warning. Failed metadata publication leaves the audio and volatile descriptor intact. Restart cannot recover text that existed only in volatile metadata, so that provenance remains unknown.
- **Normal quit drains audio.** Shutdown stops the engine, awaits preservation/closing of its recovery capture, and only then drops the reference. Accepted converter-tail/writer chunks are no longer left to race process exit.
- **Cleanup failure preserves the dictated result.** The foreground cleanup attempt has an eight-second default deadline, including remaining Apple cleanup preparation. Timeout, Use raw and provider failure select the frozen corrected raw text and bypass cleanup normalization. Late provider completion cannot replace the one-shot decision. Raw history precedes cleanup; selected text and delivery intent precede output. A noncooperative worker may drain longer, and runtime reuse waits for that drain.
- **Recovery replay keeps provenance honest.** Successful recovered text is committed before audio removal. Previously polished/combined text has nullable unknown raw provenance. Readers pull bounded chunks and continue after short reads; a short positive AVAudioFile read is not EOF.

Relevant implementation: `app/Dictate Anywhere/TranscriptionEngine.swift`, `DictationRecovery.swift`, `AppState.swift`, and the History controller/repository. No automatic provider retry, replay or paste is introduced by recovery.

## Validation status

Root reported focused r2: **129 tests, 1 skipped, 2 assertion failures**, both in one shutdown fixture that incorrectly assumed one AVAudioFile read returned every frame. The audio itself contained all **32,003 samples**, returned as **31,744 + 259** frames. The fixture was repaired to accumulate short reads; focused r3 reported **16 tests, 0 failures**. This was a test read-assumption correction, not evidence of lost tail audio.

The newly added ASR ingestion-failure and final stop-boundary writer-failure regressions are awaiting the full-suite run at this update. A synthetic **22-minute `say` replay is running; its results are pending**. No long-replay pass, completeness measurement, memory bound or completed long-session gate is claimed here.

## Remaining limits and acceptance work

1. **Total storage quota and disk-pressure policy.** The writer queue is bounded, but the overall CAF duration/bytes are not. Mono Float32 16 kHz audio alone is approximately 3.84 MB/minute. Implement/test a total recovery quota, free-space reserve and clear user-visible exhaustion policy without silently truncating or overriding History-off. Disk failure now preserves whatever was accepted and written, with an incomplete warning; it cannot reconstruct bytes the disk could not store.
2. **Full-session memory.** V2 and vocabulary configurations retain full-recording sample arrays; decoder-internal state may also grow. The pending queue and bounded replay reader do not prove a bound on those allocations. Measure duration-scaled peak memory before claiming long-session resource safety.
3. **Abrupt crash/power loss.** Normal-quit drain and orphan discovery are repairs, not a completed abrupt-termination gate. Test owned-fixture termination during recording, CAF close, metadata publication, SQLite commit and recovery replay. Validate surviving frames, database integrity, orphan classification and no automatic paste. A file that was not flushed or cannot be opened must remain an explicit incomplete/unavailable recovery case.
4. **Live capture and route pressure.** A successful file replay does not exercise microphone delivery, the 120-second pending cap, converter tails under device changes, sleep/unplug, competing inference load or writer backpressure. Run those separately with known prefix/middle/tail sentinels and injected failures; compare accepted/written/captured sample counts and observable failure state.
5. **Retention and cross-resource deletion.** Recovery retains its existing 24-hour policy. CAF/metadata deletion and SQLite transactions remain separate resources; a durable owned-file deletion/recovery queue is still later A04 work. Local pending-deletion flags do not promise persistence across restart. Filesystem snapshots/user backups are outside application deletion guarantees.
6. **Recovery capability versus transcript quality.** The 22-minute replay should report exact input duration, expected sentinels, output completeness, runtime, peak memory and artifact state. It does not establish general ASR accuracy or validate every live failure path. Recovery must never automatically re-run a provider or paste restored text.

Keep A04/A06 long-session fault-tolerance claims scoped to the tests and runtime evidence actually completed. Record the pending suite and long replay results here when root has verified them.
